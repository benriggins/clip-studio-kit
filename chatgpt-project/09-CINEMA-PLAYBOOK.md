# 09 · Cinema playbook: how a reel is directed (read before every clip plan)

You are the **director**. Claude Code is the editor, motion designer, sound designer and finisher. It can do
everything in this file and in `10-SHOT-AND-MOTION-LIBRARY.md`; your plan decides which, where, and on which word.
The brand's own look (colours, fonts, ending) is in `06-BRAND-PROFILE.md`; it overrides anything here.

## The standard: premium "business cinema", not corporate
- Feels like a short film about a business, not a slide deck. Real footage, real light, depth, sound.
- Clean: one idea on screen at a time. Nothing generic, nothing "templated", no stock-photo energy.
- Fast: cut every pause, the finished reel plays about 12% faster than the raw take. Aim for 25 to 40 seconds.

## Script shape (pick transcript lines that fit; never invent words)
| Beat | Time | Job | What usually goes on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | Stop the scroll with a contrarian or surprising line | Pattern interrupt in the first second: a cut to B-roll, a big word behind his head, or UI cards flying in. Bass hit on frame one. |
| Build | 3–20 s | Open a question and keep it open | One visual per idea: cards that get struck out, B-roll of the concrete thing he names, a counter or timer. |
| Payoff | 20–30 s | The satisfying answer ("oh, that's why I watched") | The strongest visual moment: a big word behind his head, a hero B-roll shot, a statement card landing. |
| CTA | last 2 s | One action that matches the energy | The brand's CTA (06). A small pill, never a slide. |

Re-watch rule: something new must happen every 1.5 to 3 seconds (a cut, a card, a B-roll shot, a word).

## The five passes Claude Code runs (so plan for all five)
1. **Transcribe:** every word has a timestamp. Everything you plan must name the exact words it fires on.
2. **Cut-out:** he is separated from the background on every frame, so cards and words can sit BEHIND his head.
3. **Captions:** each word appears as it's spoken; the one key word per idea goes big in a serif italic, the rest
   stay small and white. You choose the key words (`accents`, 2 to 4 per clip, single strong words are best).
4. **Motion:** B-roll, glass UI, big words, stickers, each landing on its word (library in file 10).
5. **Sound:** a hit on the hook, taps on UI pops, whooshes on cuts and moves, a chime to close, and a music bed that
   is clearly audible under the voice (viral-style instrumental, never elevator music).

## How to choose visuals (best use of each)
- **Concrete noun he says** ("a quote", "a search", "my team", "an hour") → generated **B-roll** of that exact thing.
- **A list he rejects or compares** ("not marketing, not videos…") → **glass cards** that fly in and get struck through.
- **A process or result** ("speed up operations") → **check card** with chips that tick in, or a big word behind his head.
- **A number or time** ("an hour", "four hours a week") → **timer/counter** that rolls to the number, or B-roll of it.
- **A punchline word** ("operations.", "stuck.", "tool.") → **big word behind his head**, once or twice per reel max.
- **Brands/tools he names** → **tool cards** fanned around his head, then thrown out of frame.
- Keep his face on screen for the hook line and the conviction lines. B-roll covers the explaining, 1.5 to 3 s a shot.

## Motion law (Claude Code enforces it; plan with it in mind)
- One direction for the whole reel: things arrive from the right and leave to the left, mid-motion, never from rest.
- No idle floating or wobble. If a card is on screen, it is doing something (typing, ticking, counting, loading).
- Nothing covers his eyes or mouth. Cards live at the top of the frame or around his head; captions at the bottom.
- A short pause (0.3 to 0.7 s of stillness) before the payoff lands.

## Never
- Editorial "slides" (cream pages, framed face boxes, brackets, logo bands): they read corporate.
- Coloured, outlined or ALL-CAPS "viral" captions. Captions are white; the key word is big serif italic.
- Text or numbers he didn't say. Fake results, fake clients, fake screenshots with readable fake data.
- AI images of him or of real people he names. Logos on generated people.
- Silence under the voice. Stock whooshes that are louder than the words.

## What great looks like (references the creator loved)
- Floating iOS-style cards in 3D around the speaker, some passing behind his head (alessandro.marfella).
- The "5-pass" edit: giant italic word behind the head, key-word captions, every graphic landing on a word, sound
  designed per cue (zeeuiux).
- Hook / build / payoff / CTA scripting with the payoff answering the hook (Sociyell).
