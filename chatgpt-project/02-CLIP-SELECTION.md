# Clip selection: how to find the strongest moments and build a short-form argument

> The goal is **not** to find the "best 45 seconds" inside a 45-minute video. The goal is to mine the long video
> for its strongest **idea**, then re-engineer that idea into a 30–90 second short.

Across every platform the underlying game is the same:

**Stop the swipe → create unanswered tension → keep escalating → deliver a satisfying payoff → make the viewer
want to send, share or save it.**

---

## 1. The short-form structure

| Time | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:02 | **The slap.** The most disruptive true sentence in the whole conversation | "Most companies don't actually have a marketing problem." |
| 0:02–0:08 | **Make me need the answer.** Open a question | "And spending more on marketing actually makes it worse." |
| 0:08–0:22 | **Escalation.** Evidence, story, contrast, stakes, a sharper example | "They have leads. They have traffic. What they don't have is a system that turns attention into trust…" |
| 0:22–0:36 | **The revelation.** The intellectual or emotional payoff: the part people save and send | "So the real bottleneck isn't acquisition. It's what happens between attention and conversion." |
| 0:36–0:45 | **The landing.** Finish the thought. Never trail off because the original kept talking | "Fix that system first. Then buy more attention." |

The question to ask at every second: **what unanswered reason does the viewer have to watch the next second?**

Natural speech runs *introduction → explanation → explanation → conclusion*. Short-form runs *hook → question →
partial answer → complication → bigger question → example → revelation → payoff*. Editing turns the first into
the second.

---

## 2. Don't force one continuous section: splice

A great line is often followed by 18 seconds of "and, you know, I think there's another piece to this…". Kill it.

```
31:42–31:48  "Your network doesn't become valuable when you need something…"   HOOK
30:56–31:05  "There are actually three stages of relationship capital…"        CONTEXT
32:08–32:27  "Most people start at extraction…"                               PROOF
32:40–32:48  "The strongest relationships were built before there was an ask." PAYOFF
```

Two wandering minutes become a 38-second argument, **without changing what the speaker meant**. That is excellent
clipping. Chronological order is optional; logical order is mandatory.

---

## 3. Hunt for sentence *functions*, not "viral words"

Words like "secret" or "nobody talks about this" won't save a weak idea. What works is what a sentence **does**.

| Function | Patterns hiding in transcripts |
|---|---|
| Contradiction | "Most people think X, but…" · "That's actually backwards." |
| Hidden cause | "The real reason…" · "X isn't an X problem. It's a Y problem." |
| Consequence | "And that's exactly why…" · "If you do X before Y…" |
| Mistake / confession | "The mistake I made was…" · "I did this wrong for four years." |
| Specific stakes | "We lost $___" · "It took ___ years" · "three projects" · "42%" |
| Revelation | "Then I realized…" · "What we found was…" |
| Proof | "So we tested it…" · "When we actually looked at…" |
| Framework | "There are three stages…" · "I look at every deal through four questions…" |
| Identity | "Great leaders don't…" · "If you're building a company…" |
| Before / after | "I used to believe X. Now I believe Y." |
| Narrative reversal | "We expected X. Instead, Y." |
| Compression | "The whole problem comes down to X." |
| Quotability | a line that could be the caption by itself |

**Connective words signal movement**, so lines containing them are worth a second look: *but, because, until,
except, instead, actually, which means, the problem is, here's what happened, that's when, so.*

## 4. The extraction pipeline

```
full transcript
  → semantic atoms (one thought each, often hundreds)
  → the unusually strong atoms (gold lines)
  → idea clusters (the same thesis, wherever it appears, in any take)
  → the top complete ideas
  → platform tournaments
  → for each winner: HOOK pool · CONTEXT pool · PROOF pool · MECHANISM pool · PAYOFF pool
  → the strongest possible argument, assembled from the pools
```

### Score atoms first, on six questions
1. **Surprise:** could I predict the end of the sentence from its beginning?
2. **Specificity:** numbers, names, concrete actions, outcomes?
3. **Consequence:** does something actually matter because of this?
4. **Compression:** how much meaning in how few words?
5. **Context independence:** does a stranger understand it without the previous five minutes?
6. **Charge:** does it make me feel something or rethink something?

### Then cluster gold lines by idea
Lines at 7:43, 18:22, 26:19 and 31:44 might all be pieces of one thesis ("relationship capital compounds before it
is needed"). They aren't four competing clips; they're raw material for one strong one.

### The Minimum Complete Argument
**What is the least footage a stranger needs to understand, believe and feel the payoff?**
- **Understand:** do I know what we're talking about?
- **Believe:** has enough reasoning or proof been given?
- **Payoff:** did I actually receive the promised insight?

Anything required for those three stays. Everything else goes.

### Rank with tournaments, not scores
Models bunch 1–100 scores at 87–95. Instead, run head-to-head rounds: *Idea 1 vs Idea 2: which would make the
target viewer stop?* Winner vs Idea 3… Repeat for hook strength, payoff, originality, proof, shareability and
platform fit.

## 5. What to delete, and what to protect

**Delete:**
- throat clearing ("So yeah, I mean, one thing I've kind of been thinking about…");
- duplicate statements ("like I said…");
- meta commentary ("we'll get into this later…");
- soft qualifiers that don't change the meaning;
- references to the long conversation ("going back to what Joe said…");
- setup the audience no longer needs;
- extra examples after the point is already proved.

**Protect:**
- causal bridges ("because…");
- specific details (the "400 leads");
- contrast ("we thought X, it was actually Y");
- stakes;
- the half-second breath before "and that's when I realized…";
- the antecedent of every "it", "that" and "they". Never ship Frankenstein clips where pronouns point at missing
  ideas.

---

## 6. Integrity rules (non-negotiable)

- The finished clip must mean what the speaker meant. Don't stitch fragments into a stronger claim than they made.
- Don't cut a caveat that changes the claim.
- Exact words only. If a needed sentence doesn't exist, propose a **pickup** (new line to record), clearly labelled.
- For a promising idea, write **three competing hooks** with the same body. The first sentence is often the
  difference between 20K and 2M views, but every hook must be honestly delivered by the clip.
