# Platform playbook: same footage, three different edits

Use the same core footage, but package it differently for each network.

| Platform | Starting length | Optimise hardest for | The test |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **YouTube Shorts** | 30–60 s | "Do I swipe?" + retention + payoff | *I cannot swipe yet.* |
| **Instagram Reels** | 30–60 s (up to ~90 s when earned) | stop rate + watch time + **sends** | *Who would someone DM this to?* |
| **LinkedIn** | a little over 60 s (62–75 s) | professional relevance + credibility + depth | *This changed how I think about my work.* |
| **YouTube long-form** | whatever delivers the promise | click → 30-second hold → satisfaction | see `04-YOUTUBE-LONG-FORM-PLAYBOOK.md` |

These lengths are **defaults, not caps**. The idea decides the runtime: if it resolves at 73 s, publish 73.

---

## What each platform rewards (as published by the platforms and large studies)

- **YouTube Shorts.** YouTube says Shorts ranking weighs whether viewers *choose to view* rather than swipe away,
  average view duration, average percentage viewed, likes and satisfaction, and that the first second is critical.
  Length studies disagree (one 331M-Short dataset favoured 45–59 s, another 108K-Short dataset favoured 11–20 s),
  which is good evidence that **the idea, not the duration**, decides.
- **Instagram Reels.** Instagram highlights watch time, likes and **sends** (shares by DM), with sends especially
  important for reaching non-followers. A 2026 analysis of 140K business Reels found 30–60 s had the highest average
  reach, with 60–90 s close behind. The first three seconds decide most skips.
- **LinkedIn.** LinkedIn emphasises relevance, clarity and professional usefulness; it recommends hooking in the
  first 4–6 s and calls 1–2 minutes a strong range (executive clips under ~90 s). Its own creative research found
  authentic emotion, recognisable experts and real-talk vertical video far outperform generic corporate messaging.

*(Figures as reported by the sources listed below in 2026. Re-check before quoting them publicly.)*

## Per-platform architecture

**YouTube Shorts: hunt for tension.**
`CONTRADICTION → UNANSWERED QUESTION → PROOF → COMPLICATION → REVEAL → PAYOFF`
- 0–1.5 s: the strongest claim (it should almost work without context).
- 1.5–6 s: why it's surprising.
- 6–18 s: the minimum context.
- 18–35 s: evidence and escalation.
- 35–50 s: the revelation.
- 50–58 s: the memorable conclusion.

Be the most ruthless here: delete anything that doesn't create another reason to watch.

**Instagram: hunt for recognition and sendability.**
`RELATABLE TRUTH → PERSONAL / EMOTIONAL RECOGNITION → TENSION → REFRAME → QUOTABLE LINE`
- Look for identity ("If you're building a company…"), recognition ("You ever notice how…"), relationships ("You
  don't build trust when you need something…"), emotional reframes ("What I finally realized…"), and **quotable
  lines** that could be the caption.
- Compress harder than LinkedIn. Lead with the most quotable insight.

**LinkedIn: hunt for business consequence and mechanism.**
`BUSINESS CLAIM → WHY IT MATTERS → CONTEXT / CASE → MECHANISM → PROOF / EXAMPLE → INSIGHT → BUSINESS CONSEQUENCE`
- 0–4 s: a bold professional thesis.
- 4–15 s: why a professional should care.
- 15–35 s: the problem or case.
- 35–65 s: the mechanism, data or story.
- 65–85 s: the revelation.
- 85–95 s: the implication and a memorable close.

Numbers turn opinions into cases. LinkedIn loves **because**: the reasoning, not just the claim. Let it breathe.

## Platform scoring weights

The same transcript can have a different #1 clip on every network.

| Criterion | YouTube | Instagram | LinkedIn |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hook / scroll stop | 25% | 20% | 15% |
| Curiosity / tension | 20% | 15% | 10% |
| Payoff strength | 20% | 15% | 20% |
| Shareability | 10% | 25% | 10% |
| Professional / intellectual value | 5% | 5% | 25% |
| Emotion / personality | 10% | 15% | 10% |
| Standalone coherence | 10% | 5% | 10% |

One thesis, three attack angles:
- **IG:** "Send this to somebody who's obsessed with networking."
- **LinkedIn:** "Networking is one of the worst ways to build a valuable professional network."
- **YouTube:** "Networking doesn't build your network."

---

## Two-pass workflow

1. **Content intelligence pass.** Read the whole transcript and build the thesis / argument map. Find the 10
   strongest complete ideas *before* thinking about runtime.
2. **Editor pass.** For each winning idea, find the shortest truthful version, then fit it to each platform.
   Example output: *IDEA 03, "More leads can make a broken company worse." Natural minimum 43 s. Shorts 46 s ·
   Reels 51 s · LinkedIn 88 s (adds the case study and the mechanism).*

"Make this 45 seconds" optimises for 45 seconds. You want **maximum insight per second**.
